# How Polaritium differs from Eonweld

This is a working design test, not a one-time note. Before a system goes in, check it against the table.
If a system starts to look like Eonweld's core loop, redesign the system, not just its look.

| | Eonweld | Polaritium |
|---|---|---|
| **What the player touches** | Land and sea: raise, lower, shift rivers, cause droughts | Relationships: strands between forces, their tension, phase, coupling and flow |
| **Primary verb** | Introduce a cause into geography, then watch centuries | Connect, pull, twist, pulse, tune, fracture, fuse, veil, anchor, in real time, by hand |
| **Simulation rules** | Terrain drives climate, food, population, migration, polities, belief | A field of opposing expressions (absorb / emit / mediate) with flows, oscillators, thresholds and transformations |
| **Time** | Years and centuries, an immutable historical ledger | Ticks and cycles (1 cycle = 60 ticks = 2 sim seconds); checkpoints you restore and branch, like lab notebooks |
| **What emerges** | Settlements, realms, cultures, religions, wars | Resonant rings, membranes, crystals, oscillators, alien organisms with genomes, colonies |
| **Living things** | People in towns | Abstract beings: membrane cells, motile swimmers, synchronising oscillators, adapting forms |
| **Presentation** | A painted atlas map, parchment book, Palatino | A free-flying 3D void in black, white and silver; filaments of light; no map, no terrain |
| **Progression** | History accumulates; the god's acts are priced by life | Discovery: the Atlas records real findings; Genesis wakes new laws as you find them; 12 Paradox Chambers |
| **Core appeal** | "What history follows from this landscape?" | "What happens if I try this?" — immediate tactile play with surprising physics |

## Standing checks

1. **No terrain editing as a primary power.** Environments have boundaries and character, never editable maps.
2. **No food, farms, villages, kingdoms, trade, wars between nations.** Organisms eat radiance or vibration and that is all.
3. **No chronological history book.** The Atlas records discoveries; the event log is a short rolling list for the
   inspector. Neither is the defining mechanic.
4. **No code, assets or designs from Eonweld.** Nothing is imported or copied from `35 Eonweld/`. Generic
   engineering practice (seeded randomness, save files, tests) is shared practice, not Eonweld's design.
5. **The player's hands are on the relationships.** If a new feature can be played without touching a strand,
   a force or a field, ask whether it belongs.
